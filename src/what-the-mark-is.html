What the Mark Is, and What It Costs

Home › Stand Firm › What the Mark Is

Of all the images in the Book of Revelation, the mark of the beast is the one people ask me about most. They are right to ask. It is the one point in chapter 13 where the reader is no longer only watching. Here the reader has to choose.

What Scripture says

The second beast “forced all the people, small and great, rich and poor, free and slave, to be given a stamped image on their right hands or their foreheads, so that no one could buy or sell except one who had the stamped image of the beast's name or the number that stood for its name” (Revelation 13:16–17).

Three things stand out. The mark is universal: no one is too small or too great to be asked. It is forced: it is pressed on people, not freely invented by them. And it is tied to daily life: without it no one can buy or sell.

Then comes the warning, the most solemn in the book: “Anyone who worships the beast or its image, or accepts its mark on forehead or hand, will also drink the wine of God's fury” (Revelation 14:9–10). Notice how worship and the mark are joined. The mark is not a tattoo that falls on someone by accident. It is the outward sign of an inward surrender, the seal of a worship given to the beast that belongs to God alone.

What Scripture does not say

Scripture does not tell us what the mark is made of. It might be a chip, a digital ID, a currency, an oath or a document. The Fathers read it in different ways. St. Irenaeus took it literally. St. Augustine read the forehead as what we profess and the hand as what we do (City of God XX.9). We should not frighten anyone by calling every new technology the mark. A technology becomes the mark only when it is joined to the worship of the beast. As I have written before, we will recognize it when we realize that we cannot buy or sell without it, and that to receive it we must worship the Antichrist.

Nor does the mark fall on anyone without their knowing. God does not trick his children into damnation. Those who receive it choose it, whether out of fear, for gain, or because they have been deceived and did not love the truth (2 Thessalonians 2:10).

What it costs

To accept the mark costs everything: it means giving the beast the allegiance that belongs to God. To refuse it may cost a great deal in this life. Fr. Charles Arminjon, writing in 1881, imagined it plainly. Those who bear the mark will enjoy high salaries, public offices and every comfort. Those who refuse will be shut out, even “forbidden to draw water from the public fountains” (The End of the Present World, p. 49).

That is why the mark has to be understood before it is offered. When the moment comes there may be little time to think. Families will need to eat, jobs will be at stake, and the pressure will be enormous. The decision we make then will be the decision we have been preparing for, day by day, all our lives.

The other seal

Revelation never leaves us with only the beast's mark. Before the trial begins, God's servants are sealed on their foreheads (Revelation 7:3), and the Lamb's followers bear “his name and his Father's name” (Revelation 14:1). We were sealed already at Baptism and Confirmation. The question is not whether we will be marked, but whose mark we will bear.

“Here is what sustains the holy ones who keep God's commandments and their faith in Jesus” (Revelation 14:12).
