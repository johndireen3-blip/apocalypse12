The Three Frogs

Three unclean spirits from three mouths, sent to the kings of the whole world. I propose three men through whom those spirits may be speaking, and I hold these names with less confidence than anything else on this site.

Home › The Possibility › The Three Frogs

Where this page standsThis is Fr. John's proposal, not the Church's teaching. The Church has never said who or what the three frogs are. Each name below is graded possible, none is settled, and each comes with the case against it. Words and deeds are weighed here, never souls (CCC 1861). No one's religion, people or family is used as evidence. Nothing on this page invites hostility toward anyone named.

The text

"I saw three unclean spirits like frogs come from the mouth of the dragon, from the mouth of the beast, and from the mouth of the false prophet. These were demonic spirits who performed signs. They went out to the kings of the whole world to assemble them for the battle on the great day of God the almighty" (Revelation 16:13–14).

The text gives three things to look for, and this page tests each candidate against them:

  • A mouth. Each spirit comes out of a mouth: the dragon's, the beast's, the false prophet's. The spirit carries what that mouth speaks.
  • Signs. The spirits perform signs (16:14).
  • The kings. They go out to the kings of the whole world, to assemble them for battle (16:14, 16).

How the Church's writers have read it

  • St. Bede (d. 735) reads the three as one evil at work on three levels: the spirit of the devil, of the Antichrist, and of those set over the Antichrist's body. He explains the frogs by their croaking and by the filth and mud they live in, and he recalls Egypt: Pharaoh's magicians "were permitted to prevail by their enchantments as far as the sign of frogs." The kings, he says, are the evil rulers who fight against the saints, each in his own nation (Explanation of the Apocalypse, on chapter 16).
  • The Haydock commentary (1859) takes the frogs as devils sent out to stir up the wicked, and Armageddon as a name for a place of great destruction, not a spot on a map (on Apocalypse 16).
  • The New American Bible note points back to the plague of frogs (Exodus 7:26–8:11) and identifies the false prophet with the second beast of Revelation 13.
  • A modern study observes that in the ancient world frogs stood for endless, empty talk, and that St. John uses the image to mock the powers he describes (Stephan Witetschek, New Testament Studies, 2008).

Two things follow for this page. First, the tradition reads the frogs as spirits and as speech: the unclean thing is what comes out of the mouth. Second, no Father or approved commentator names three individuals. Naming three men is my proposal, and it goes beyond them.

My reading

I do not say that any man is a demon. The text says the frogs are demonic spirits, and I leave that as it stands. What I propose is narrower: that a lying spirit works through human mouths, as it did through the prophets of Ahab (1 Kings 22:22), and that in our time the voice of each of the three mouths may be heard in the public words of one man who speaks for it. I name the three by what they have said and done in public. If I am wrong about a name, the text loses nothing.

My first version of this page named Mike Johnson, the Speaker of the House, as the voice from the mouth of the beast. I now propose Stephen Miller in his place, and I give the reasons under the second frog below.

1. From the mouth of the dragon: Elon Musk

The text. The dragon is "the ancient serpent, who is called the Devil and Satan, who deceived the whole world" (Revelation 12:9). His mouth has already appeared once: the serpent "spewed a torrent of water out of his mouth after the woman to sweep her away with the current" (Revelation 12:15). What comes from the dragon's mouth is a flood, and its purpose is to deceive.

My reading. No man in history has owned a larger mouth. Elon Musk bought one of the world's main channels of public speech, speaks through it daily to hundreds of millions, and has built a machine that speaks in his company's name to governments. Unlike my other two candidates he holds no office under the first beast and has quarreled with him in public, which is why I assign him to the dragon's mouth and not the beast's. I grade this possible.

A flood of words, and what is in it.

  • He was the largest individual donor of the 2024 election, at $291 million, and has given more than $90 million to his party's side in 2026. During the 2024 campaign he promoted falsehoods about election fraud and immigration on his own platform, and a committee he funded ran advertisements that misrepresented his opponent's positions (Wikipedia, with its sources).
  • On March 20, 2026, a San Francisco jury found him liable for misleading investors in two of his own posts (same).
  • On February 28, 2025, he said: "The fundamental weakness of Western civilization is empathy" (Snopes). Christ's command is the opposite: "Be merciful, just as your Father is merciful" (Luke 6:36).
  • In February 2025, as his office dismantled the United States' foreign aid agency, he told a summit of governments in Dubai that his team would "delete entire agencies" (Daily Beast). Bill Gates accused him of "killing the world's poorest children" (Wikipedia). The Church counts feeding the hungry among the works of mercy (CCC 2447).

Signs.

  • In 2014 he said of artificial intelligence: "With artificial intelligence we're summoning the demon" (TechCrunch). He then built one. I quote the words as his own image, not as proof.
  • His chatbot, Grok, has been criticized for praising Adolf Hitler, for antisemitic tropes and for producing sexualized images of women and children without consent (Wikipedia, with its sources). It bears on the image that speaks.

To the kings, for battle.

  • He has spoken into the politics of other nations. On January 25, 2025, he told a rally of Germany's Alternative für Deutschland: "There is too much focus on past guilt, and we need to move beyond that" (Axios). He has intervened in Britain's politics, and in February 2026 called Spain's prime minister a "tyrant" (Wikipedia).
  • TIME reported in October 2026 that at an undisclosed meeting with him in December 2025, the president spent hours questioning Grok, asked it how Venezuelans would respond to the capture of their president, and a month later ordered the operation that seized him. Two ranking members of Congress have asked that the records be preserved (TIME, Oct 30, 2026). The account rests on unnamed officials, and I hold it as reported, not established.
  • On September 30, 2026, the Secretary of Defense named him a leader of Project Meridian, a Pentagon study of "the future of warfare" that is to identify the domains the United States "must conquer." His companies already build the military's satellite networks, and Grok is cleared for its classified systems (Euronews).

The case against.

  • The dragon is Satan. To say that a man speaks for the dragon's mouth is the gravest proposal on this page, and nothing here shows it. Loud, false or cruel speech is common to fallen men and does not need a demon to explain it.
  • In June 2025 he broke with the president, called his chief bill a "disgusting abomination" and called for his impeachment. The three frogs work as one. A man who publicly fought the first beast fits that poorly, even though the two have since reconciled.
  • His words on empathy have a context: in the same conversation he said, "I believe in empathy, like, I think you should care about other people," and argued against what he called its misuse. He calls himself a "cultural Christian."
  • He has warned against artificial intelligence for years. He gave Ukraine satellite service at his own cost. Cutting a government agency is a policy judgment on which people differ, and estimates of the deaths that followed are models, not counts.
  • Rockets, satellites and chatbots are engineering. Bede's signs are enchantments that deceive, like those of Pharaoh's magicians. No wonder of his has been presented to the world as a sign calling for the beast's worship.
  • Speaking at a foreign party's rally is political speech, which is not a summons of kings to battle.

Would strengthen: his machines or his platform used to turn rulers and peoples against the Church, or presented as wonders that win honor for the first beast. Would weaken: a lasting break with the first beast; his platform turned to the service of truth and mercy. Standing: possible.

2. From the mouth of the beast: Stephen Miller

The text. "The beast was given a mouth uttering proud boasts and blasphemies" (Revelation 13:5). The second frog comes out of that mouth.

Why I changed the name. I first proposed Mike Johnson, because as Speaker he carried the first beast's program through Congress and spoke for it there. The Speaker's office is not the beast's mouth, and it passes from him in January 2027 after his party lost the House. Stephen Miller has a closer claim to the text than any officeholder: for ten years he has written the words that the first beast speaks.

My reading. If the first beast has a mouth, Stephen Miller has been its pen and often its voice. He wrote the speeches, shaped the policies they announced, and now directs the staff that carries them out. I grade this possible.

The beast's words, written by him.

  • He joined the campaign in January 2016 and became its principal speechwriter. He wrote the speech that closed the 2016 convention (Duke Chronicle), and the White House acknowledged that he and Steve Bannon wrote much of the 2017 inaugural address, with its "American carnage" (Wikipedia, citing Jean Guerrero's Hatemonger; Mediaite).
  • He is described on every side as the architect of the mass-deportation policy weighed on The First Beast, card 7. On November 6, 2026, he became White House chief of staff and wrote: "the mission now is more clear than ever: Fight with all of our soul to support our President" (Politico). Scripture reserves that measure for God: "you shall love the LORD, your God, with your whole heart, and with your whole being, and with your whole strength" (Deuteronomy 6:5).

His own words.

  • About 900 emails he sent to editors at Breitbart in 2015 and 2016, given to the Southern Poverty Law Center by a former editor there, show him urging coverage of The Camp of the Saints, a novel of racial invasion, and passing along material from VDARE (SPLC, Nov 12, 2019).
  • At the memorial for Charlie Kirk on September 21, 2025, he addressed those he called the forces of wickedness: "You have no idea the dragon you have awakened," and, "You have nothing. You are nothing" (The New Republic; also Baptist News Global). The image of the dragon was his own choice. I note it and build nothing on a figure of speech.
  • Terry Moran's judgment of him, given at the cost of his post at ABC News, is set out on The First Beast, card 7.

To the kings.

  • On CNN in January 2026, pressing the claim to Greenland, he said: "We live in a world, in the real world... that is governed by strength, that is governed by force, that is governed by power. These are the iron laws of the world since the beginning of time," and, "Nobody is going to fight the United States militarily over the future of Greenland" (Axios; Wikipedia). The world's question about the beast is, "who can fight against it?" (Revelation 13:4). Denmark's prime minister answered that if the United States attacks a NATO ally, "then everything stops."
  • On Fox News on May 20, 2026, during the war with Iran, he said its rulers could agree to terms satisfactory to the United States, or "they can face a punishment from our military the likes of which has not been seen in modern history" (Iran International).

The case against.

  • Speechwriters serve every ruler. Writing a president's words is an ordinary office, and the words remain the president's. A scribe is not a spirit.
  • The text gives the frogs signs. I can point to none of his.
  • The frogs gather the kings of the whole world. He threatens foreign rulers; he does not assemble them. His work has been chiefly at home.
  • "White nationalist" is an author's description and the extremist listing is an advocacy group's label. Neither is a finding. Forwarding articles is not the same as holding every view in them, and he and his defenders describe his policy as enforcing the law, which nations may do (CCC 2241).
  • Strong words at a memorial for a murdered friend are grief and anger, not doctrine. "With all of our soul" is the common language of political devotion, and it was a pledge he offered, not a demand the president made.
  • Moran's words are one man's judgment and cited no specific act. A Senator of his own party called the Greenland remarks "amateurish"; that is a judgment of policy, not of spirit.

Would strengthen: his words turned plainly against God, the Church or her sacraments; rulers of other nations rallied by him to the first beast's side. Would weaken: his removal or a break with the first beast; policy turned toward mercy for the stranger. Standing: possible.

3. From the mouth of the false prophet: Steve Witkoff

The text. The false prophet is the second beast, who "had two horns like a lamb's but spoke like a dragon" and "wielded all the authority of the first beast in its sight" (Revelation 13:11–12; 19:20). Of the three frogs, this is the one whose work the text describes most exactly: they "went out to the kings of the whole world" (16:14).

My reading. I propose Jared Kushner as the false prophet. Steve Witkoff is his partner in office: the two share the title of Special Envoy for Peace Missions, travel together, and speak together to the rulers of the earth. Where Kushner goes in person or by video, Witkoff is the one in the room. I grade this possible, and of the three it is the one that fits the text's own description best.

Out to the kings of the whole world.

  • A real-estate developer and friend of the president with no diplomatic experience, he was named envoy to the Middle East in November 2024 and has become the president's channel to Vladimir Putin, meeting him at least eight times between February 2025 and October 2026 (TASS, to December 2025; Wikipedia). He has also dealt with the rulers of Saudi Arabia, the Emirates, Qatar and Israel, and with Iran's foreign minister.
  • After meeting Putin in March 2025 he said: "I don't regard Putin as a bad guy," called him "super smart," and reported that Putin had prayed for the president and commissioned his portrait (same, citing Newsweek and CNN).
  • At the Constantine Palace in St. Petersburg on October 21, 2026, after Russia released an American prisoner, he told Putin: "You are a humanitarian also, sir, and I salute that. I commend you" (Al Jazeera). In 2023 the International Criminal Court issued a warrant for Putin's arrest over the unlawful deportation of children from Ukraine.

Speaking for more than one side.

  • A transcript published by Bloomberg in November 2025 shows him advising Putin's aide Yuri Ushakov, in a call on October 14, 2025, on how Putin should approach the president: "I would make the call and just reiterate that you congratulate the president on this achievement." The president called it "a standard thing"; Putin's envoy Kirill Dmitriev called the transcript fake (Fox 11).
  • Axios reported that he drafted the 28-point plan of November 2025, which asked Ukraine to give up territory and limit its army, together with Dmitriev. A congressman of the president's own party, Don Bacon, wrote that he "fully favors the Russians" (Wikipedia, with its sources).

For battle.

  • With Kushner he led the talks with Iran in early 2026. They ended on February 28, 2026, when the United States and Israel struck Iran. I have set out that sequence, talks of peace and then fire from the sky, on The Second Beast, card 9. Diplomats later said he had misrepresented a key exchange in those talks (Wikipedia, citing MS NOW, March 3, 2026).

Peace and profit.

  • His family co-founded the cryptocurrency firm World Liberty Financial with the president's sons; his son is its chief executive. The New York Times reported that another son sought investment from several of the governments his father was negotiating with. The Wall Street Journal reported that a firm tied to the Emirates' Sheikh Tahnoon secretly held 49 percent of it, and Reuters that a Tahnoon-linked firm placed $2 billion in its currency. Forbes puts his fortune at $2.3 billion, grown during his time in office (Wikipedia; Slate, July 22, 2026). It bears on buying and selling.

The case against.

  • "Blessed are the peacemakers" (Matthew 5:9). He helped bring about ceasefires in Gaza in January and October 2025, hostages came home, and at his meeting with Putin on October 21, 2026, Russia released an American prisoner. Seeking peace between nations is good, and the Church asks for it.
  • The frogs gather kings for battle. An envoy who gathers them for talks is doing the opposite on its face, and a war that follows failed talks is not shown to be his purpose.
  • Kind words to a ruler across the table are the ordinary method of diplomacy. The president called the Ushakov call standard negotiation, and no inquiry has found otherwise.
  • He was sworn in and, the president says, reports to the Secretary of State. The White House says he has divested from World Liberty Financial and has no conflict of interest. Nothing unlawful has been found.
  • The text gives the frogs signs. I can point to none of his.
  • This reading depends on my reading of the false prophet. If that is wrong, this falls with it.

Would strengthen: rulers drawn through his missions into a common front that turns on the Church or on those who will not honor the first beast; proof of the reported private gain. Would weaken: a just peace that holds; his missions shown to serve only his country's lawful interests. Standing: possible.

The three together

Set against the text's three tests, the proposal stands like this:

The weakest point is the same for all three: the signs. Until something like a sign appears, none of these names can rise above possible, and I say so. The text also places the frogs at the sixth bowl, late in the story Revelation tells. I set no date and do not claim that hour has come (CCC 673).

St. John pictures these spirits as frogs, small and noisy, and the next words in the text are Christ's own: "Behold, I am coming like a thief. Blessed is the one who watches" (Revelation 16:15). The frogs are given so that we may not be taken in by a flood of words, not so that we may fear them. See Stand Firm.
