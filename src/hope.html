Where hope comes from

Pope St. John Paul II never stopped proclaiming the value of hope. On September 15, 1987, he reminded the young people of Los Angeles:

“Hope comes from God, from our belief in God. People of hope are those who believe God created them for a purpose and that he will provide for their needs. They believe that God loves them as a faithful Father. Do you remember the advice that Jesus gave his disciples when they seemed to be fearful of the future? He said: ‘Do not be concerned for your life, what you are to eat, or for your body, what you are to wear. Life is more important than food and the body more than clothing. Consider the ravens: they do no[t] sow, they do not reap, they have neither cellar nor barn – yet God feeds them. How much more important you are than the birds!’ (Luke 12:22–24). Yes, God knows all our needs. He is the foundation for our hope.”Pope St. John Paul II, Teleconference with the youth of Los Angeles, September 15, 1987

Hope in Revelation 12

Revelation 12 is a chapter of hope before it is a chapter of war. Before the dragon can do any harm, “the woman herself fled into the desert where she had a place prepared by God, that there she might be taken care of for twelve hundred and sixty days” (Revelation 12:6). When the serpent pursues her, she is “given the two wings of the great eagle” and is taken care of again (Revelation 12:14). And before the dragon's war on her children has even begun, heaven announces how it ends: “Now have salvation and power come, and the kingdom of our God and the authority of his Anointed” (Revelation 12:10). The trial is real, but God provides for his own through it, and the victory is announced before the battle.

A virtue for the time of trial

“Hope is the theological virtue by which we desire the kingdom of heaven and eternal life as our happiness, placing our trust in Christ's promises and relying not on our own strength, but on the help of the grace of the Holy Spirit” (CCC 1817). Hope “keeps man from discouragement; it sustains him during times of abandonment” (CCC 1818). St. Paul shows how it grows in suffering: “affliction produces endurance, and endurance, proven character, and proven character, hope, and hope does not disappoint, because the love of God has been poured out into our hearts through the holy Spirit that has been given to us” (Romans 5:3–5).

The one who has hope lives differently

Pope Benedict XVI wrote that “the one who has hope lives differently; the one who hopes has been granted the gift of a new life” (Spe Salvi 2). That is why this site ends with hope, not with the beast. Whatever one concludes about Revelation 13, the Woman's Son has already won, and the pages above are the help God gives us to share in his victory.
