A question readers ask

Is Donald Trump the Antichrist?

My short answer: no one can say that he is, and the Church has named no one. I propose it as a possibility, and I test it against Revelation 13 mark by mark.

Home › The Evidence › Is Donald Trump the Antichrist?

The short answer

The Church teaches that before Christ returns she must pass through a final trial, and that its supreme deception is that of the Antichrist (CCC 675). She has never identified the Antichrist with anyone living, and nothing on this site speaks for her. Many rulers have been named over the centuries, and every confident naming so far has failed.

What I offer is a proposal, made in my own name as a priest: that Donald J. Trump is the first beast of Revelation 13, the ruler the tradition calls the Antichrist. I do not say it is certain. I grade each mark of the beast possible or probable, never settled, and beside each I set the evidence against my own reading. If the proposal is wrong, I want it shown to be wrong.

How I grade it

Revelation does not give a name. It gives marks. These are the marks of the first beast, with my grade for each and where it stands today.

No mark is settled, and none is fully seen.

The strongest evidence

  • The wound and the return. Revelation's beast has a head that "seemed to have been mortally wounded, but this mortal wound was healed," and the world followed in amazement (13:3). He lost the presidency, was written off, and took it back four years later.
  • The mouth. The beast is given "a mouth uttering proud boasts and blasphemies" (13:5). "I alone can fix it." "I am the chosen one." In April 2026, in the days he attacked Pope Leo XIV, an image of himself robed like Christ and healing the sick appeared on his own account.
  • The seven heads, and an eighth. The beast "is an eighth king, but really belongs to the seven" (17:11). He is the forty-fifth president and the forty-seventh: one man, two reigns, the second belonging to the first.

The strongest case against

  • No one is made to worship him. Revelation's beast is worshiped by "all the inhabitants of the earth," and those who refuse are killed (13:8, 15). His honors are political. No one is persecuted for the faith as such, and the courts have protected the Church.
  • He does not hold the world. The beast is given authority over "every tribe, people, tongue, and nation" (13:7). Most nations resist him, and in the midterm elections of November 2026 his own country gave both houses of Congress to the other party.
  • The tradition does not read it this way. No Father or Doctor reads the seven heads as I do. Boasting is common to many rulers and is not yet blasphemy, and each episode touching God was disclaimed. A political comeback is not a healed mortal wound.
  • History counsels caution. St. Irenaeus judged it "more certain, and less hazardous, to await the fulfilment of the prophecy, than to be making surmises" (Against Heresies V.30.3). Those who named Nero, Napoleon and many others were wrong.

What I do not say

  • I do not judge his soul. Words and deeds are weighed here, never souls; that judgment belongs to God alone (CCC 1861). We are certainly called to pray for our leaders (1 Timothy 2:1–2), but keep in mind the admonition of St. John (1 John 5:16).
  • I do not judge those who voted for him. Supporting a candidate is not worshiping a beast.
  • I set no date. "It is not for you to know the times or seasons" (Acts 1:7; CCC 673).

What is asked of us

Whoever the beast proves to be, the faithful are asked the same thing in every age: to worship God alone, to refuse what cannot be given to any ruler, and to endure. "Such is the faithfulness and endurance of the holy ones" (Revelation 13:10). See Stand Firm and Hope.
