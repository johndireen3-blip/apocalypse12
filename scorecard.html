Part III · The evidence

The Scorecard: Watching in Real Time

A proposal that can never be wrong cannot be tested. Here Fr. John's reading is held to Scripture one mark at a time, with the evidence against set down as faithfully as the evidence for.

Home › The Evidence

A work in progressThis scorecard is updated as events unfold and as Fr. John sets out his reasons. Cells marked “Not yet stated” await his reading; cells marked “Evidence to be cited” await sourced evidence. No row says that anyone is the Antichrist: the grades stay possible or probable, and judgment of persons belongs to God alone (CCC 1861).

How the scorecard works

Each row takes one mark: something Scripture says about the beasts and the frogs. It gives Fr. John's reading of how the mark is met, the evidence so far on each side, what would strengthen or weaken the reading, and where it stands now. Only what is on the public record or in Fr. John's own writing is entered; current events are added only with a cited, reputable source.

StandingMeaning
SeenThe mark is plainly met, on evidence a skeptical reader would accept.
Partly seenSome features are met, others not yet, or the fit depends on Fr. John's reading.
Not yet seenNothing so far meets it. This is expected for marks of the final stage, and does not count against the proposal unless the order is broken (see the checks below).
AgainstThe evidence or the text weighs against Fr. John's reading.
To assessEvidence still to be gathered and sourced.

Rules for evidence. Each item gives the claim, its source and how reliable it is, the mark it bears on, and whether it strengthens or weakens the case. Fringe sources appear only as low-weight “claims circulating,” never as evidence. Words and deeds are tested, never souls. Political loyalty, admiration or a vote is never counted as worship or as the mark. Evidence against is entered as faithfully as evidence for.

Checks set in advance

These checks are written down now, before events, so that readers can hold the proposal to them. None of them names a date. They draw on Scripture's own order and on the Fathers and Fr. Charles Arminjon. A prophecy that does not come true is “a word the LORD did not speak” (Deuteronomy 18:22).

  1. Universal rule before the end of the Mass. Arminjon holds that the Mass ceases only after the Antichrist has universal dominion (The End of the Present World, p. 51). If public Masses were suppressed without such rule, that would be another foreshadowing, not the fulfillment. If universal rule never comes, the proposal is weakened.
  2. Worship before the mark. The mark is joined to worship (Revelation 13:15–17; 14:9). A control system without worship is not the mark, and does not by itself strengthen the proposal.
  3. The world marvels at the healing. Revelation says the whole earth followed the beast in amazement after the wound was healed (Revelation 13:3). If the return to power is not met with something beyond ordinary politics, the reading of the wound is weakened.
  4. The second beast's order. Signs (Revelation 13:13) come before the image (13:14–15), and the image before the mark (13:16–17).
  5. The witnesses during his reign. Revelation 11 places two witnesses in the time of the beast; the Fathers and Arminjon expect them before his fall (pp. 52–56).
  6. The decisive check. The proposal stands or falls on the marks of the final stage: universal reach (7), war on the holy ones (8), worship demanded (5), the image (15) and the mark (16). If he leaves power by ordinary means without these having appeared, the proposal is weakened decisively, and this site will say so.
Read with careThese checks are for testing a reading, not for watching a man for his downfall. His fate, like ours, is in God's hands (Revelation 19:20; CCC 1861). Nothing on this site invites anyone to act against anyone.
