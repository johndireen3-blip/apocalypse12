They Will Seek Death and Not Find It

Transhumanism promises a life without death, made by human hands. I watch it beside two texts: the people who "will seek death but will not find it" (Revelation 9:6), and the second beast, who gives breath to a made thing (Revelation 13:15).

Where this page standsThis is Fr. John's reading, not the Church's teaching, and it is the lightest mark on this site: something to watch, graded possible and no more. No Father reads Revelation 9:6 this way, and the technology it supposes does not exist. Words and deeds are weighed here, never souls (CCC 1861). Medicine that heals and lengthens life is a good, and nothing on this page is said against it or against anyone who hopes in it.

What transhumanism is

By transhumanism I mean the hope of overcoming the limits of human nature, and death above all, by technique: medicine, machines and the remaking of the body, so that earthly life could be prolonged without end. It is a current of thought with many voices and no single author. Caring for one's health, and medicine that cures and lengthens life, are not transhumanism; the Church calls life and health precious gifts to be cared for reasonably (CCC 2288). What I watch is the promise that death itself can be abolished by human power.

The texts

Revelation 9. At the fifth trumpet the shaft of the abyss is opened and locusts come out of its smoke. They may harm only those who do not have the seal of God on their foreheads, and they may not kill them, only torment them for five months (Revelation 9:1–5). Then: these people "will seek death but will not find it"; they long to die, and death escapes them (Revelation 9:6).

Revelation 13. The second beast has an image made for the first beast and is permitted "to breathe life" into it, so that it speaks (Revelation 13:14–15). He serves a beast whose mortal wound was healed, a mimicry of the Lord's death and Resurrection (Revelation 13:3, 12).

My reading

I ask whether a deathless life made by human hands could become the life Revelation 9:6 describes: a life one cannot leave. And I watch it beside the second beast, because his work in Revelation 13 is to give a counterfeit of life, breath to a made thing, in the service of a beast whose healing counterfeits the Resurrection. A promise of life without death, and without God, would be that same counterfeit offered to everyone. The true promise is different in kind: "whoever believes in me, even if he dies, will live" (John 11:25). Christ does not abolish death by technique. He passes through it and opens it.

I grade this mark possible. It is a watch, not a claim that anything is fulfilled.

What the Church teaches

  • Death entered the world through sin, and Christ has transformed it: because of him Christian death has a positive meaning, as the passage to life with God (CCC 1008–1010). The Christian hope is the resurrection of the body, God's gift, not the indefinite prolonging of this life (CCC 997–1001).
  • On December 10, 2025, in a catechesis on the Lord's Pasch as the answer to the question of our death, Pope Leo XIV spoke of views of man that promise an immortality within this world through technology, called this the transhuman scenario, and counted it among the challenges of our time. He asked: "Could death really be defeated by science?" And he asked further whether science could guarantee that a life without death would also be a happy one (General Audience, December 10, 2025). He named no person and no company.
  • On November 13, 2026, at Chiclayo, he said that scientific progress must not be confused with a privilege for the wealthy who seek immortality while whole peoples suffer; his words are quoted in full on The Second Beast, card 10. Again he named no one.

How the Church's writers have read Revelation 9:6

None of the early commentators reads the verse of a technology or of a life prolonged by human skill.

  • Tyconius takes it in a good sense: these seek death as rest from evil, wishing to die to the world and to live for God (Romans 6:11).
  • Andrew of Caesarea takes it as the measure of the affliction: people in great distress call on death, and by God's judgment it does not come, so that the bitterness may make sin hateful to them.
  • Oecumenius places the whole chapter after the resurrection, as the punishment of sinners. He is the nearest neighbor to my reading, since he too sees people who suffer and cannot die, but his setting is the life to come, not this one.

Source: Revelation, ed. William C. Weinrich, Ancient Christian Commentary on Scripture, New Testament XII (InterVarsity Press, 2005), on 9:6; summarized on The Lamb's Victory, chapter 9.

What has been seen

  • The idea is abroad. It is current enough that the Pope has twice addressed it, in December 2025 and in November 2026 (above).
  • A remark by the man I propose as the second beast. In August 2022, in a livestream for his memoir, Jared Kushner said he had made exercise a priority, and that with the advances in science there was "a good probability" that his generation would be "the first generation to live forever or the last generation that's going to die" (HuffPost via AOL, August 2022).

The case against

Against the reading of Revelation 9:6.

  • No Father reads it this way. Tyconius, Andrew and Oecumenius each take it otherwise.
  • The text limits it. The torment lasts five months and falls on those without the seal of God. It is a plague sent upon people, not a condition they built for themselves and then regretted.
  • The cause is different. In the verse death flees from them; nothing says they first sought to escape it.
  • The technology does not exist. No one has been made unable to die. The reading explains an aspiration, not an event.

Against tying it to the second beast.

  • It was one remark, more than four years ago, hedged with "hopefully" and made while speaking of exercise. A source close to him told The Daily Beast it was a "tongue-in-cheek joke" about people living longer (The Daily Beast, August 2022).
  • Hoping that medicine will lengthen life is common and is not transhumanism. Nothing shows him funding, building or preaching a project to abolish death.
  • Revelation 13 gives the second beast signs, an image, a mark and enforced worship. It says nothing of a promise of long life. The link to 13:15 is my own inference.
  • The Pope's warnings are addressed to a current of thought and name no one.

Would strengthen: a promise of life without death preached as a salvation, above all if joined to allegiance to a ruler; the second beast's hand in such a project, by his money, his companies or his word. Would weaken: the remark standing alone, with nothing after it; the hope of abolishing death fading as its promises fail. Standing: not yet seen.

What is asked of us

Not fear. The Pope's own answer to this hope is not alarm but Easter: death is not the opposite of life, and Christ has made it a passage. We are asked to prepare for a good death, to care for the sick and the dying, and to hope for the resurrection that only God can give. See Hope and Stand Firm.
