The pages "as first written" keep my proposal exactly as I stated it, beginning in August 2023, before any critical review: the Antichrist, the false prophet and 666 and the mark. Later revisions to the weighed pages are recorded here too. I leave them unchanged so that readers can see where I started. This page is where they are tested. Each entry gives the passage, the objection and its source, my reply or the revision I have accepted, and where the point stands now. Entries will be added as the review continues.

1. The first beast: his conception and the atomic age

As first written. I tied his conception, "days after the bombing of Hiroshima and Nagasaki," to a "portal to Hell," and quoted St. John Damascene, through Fr. Arminjon, that the Antichrist's birth "will be saturated with the breath and spirit of Satan."

The objection. Applied to one man, those words judge his soul, and read literally they say something about his parents, who were married. These sites weigh words and deeds, never souls (CCC 1861). The timing was also off: born on June 14, 1946, he was conceived about a month after the bombings, not days.

The revision I accept.

Donald J. Trump was born on June 14, 1946, and so was conceived in the weeks after the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki, at the start of an age in which rulers would hold power over the life of the whole world. Tradition speaks of the Antichrist's origin in vivid terms: "He will be a child of fornication," says St. John Damascene, "and his birth will be saturated with the breath and spirit of Satan" (Fr. Charles Arminjon, The End of the Present World, p. 43). Damascene wrote this of the Antichrist, not of any man living, and I do not apply its words to Trump's parents or to his soul. I note the timing as a sign of the age he was born into.

Why him, and not the millions of others conceived in those same months? No one can see that at a cradle. If it can be seen at all, it is only by looking back from what a life became. In the desert Satan offered Christ "all the kingdoms of the world" if he would worship him (Matthew 4:8–9), and Christ refused. In Revelation the dragon gives the beast "its own power and throne, along with great authority" (Revelation 13:2). Satan offers; a man accepts or refuses, and no one is forced. Of all those born in that season, he is the one who rose to the most powerful office in the world, lost it, and took it back. It is in his public words and deeds, set out mark by mark on The Evidence, that I propose the offer was accepted. That is an observation about a public life, which anyone can test, not a verdict on a soul, which belongs to God alone.

Where it stands. Revised; accepted November 1, 2026. The evidence is weighed on The First Beast.

2. The second beast: "the earth" and his faith

As first written. I wrote that many read "the earth" as Israel or religion, and ended his description: "He practices Modern Orthodox Judaism."

The objection. Set beside "Israel," his faith reads as evidence: as if being Jewish were a sign of the false prophet. The Church teaches that the Jewish people are not to be presented as rejected or accursed, and deplores every form of antisemitism (Nostra Aetate 4); Standard 28 on Apocalypse 13 holds every reading to this. The reading of "the earth" as Israel is real but held by a minority: some popular futurist sources (BibleRef) and preterists who apply it to the high priests of Jerusalem before AD 70 (Kenneth Gentry). Most commentators read the second beast as the religious machinery that serves state power; in Revelation 13 the same word means the whole world (13:12, 14); and where some Fathers spoke of Jewish descent, they said it of the Antichrist, not of the false prophet.

The revision I accept. I make the argument from his role, not his faith. Revelation's second beast is the servant who acts "in the sight of" the first beast, wields "all the authority of the first beast," and gives that power a lamb's face and a religious meaning (13:11–12). No one stands closer to the first beast or acts so fully in his name: son-in-law, adviser, and envoy without an office of his own. "The earth" I read as the religious sphere and the Holy Land, where his work has been centered: peace given the name of Abraham, the patriarch of three faiths; the embassy in Jerusalem; the Board of Peace; and a decades-long closeness to Israel's prime minister. His faith is part of his life and is respected here, but it is not part of the evidence. The case is set out, with the evidence against it, on The Second Beast.

Where it stands. Revised; accepted November 4, 2026.

3. The three frogs: Stephen Miller in place of Mike Johnson

As first proposed. I named Mike Johnson, the Speaker of the House, as the unclean spirit from the mouth of the beast (Revelation 16:13), because as Speaker he carried the first beast's program through Congress and spoke for it there.

The objection. The text says the spirit comes "from the mouth of the beast." A Speaker holds an office of his own, given by the House and not by the president, and it passes from him in January 2027 after his party lost the House in the 2026 midterms. An office that an election can take away is not the beast's mouth.

The revision I accept. I now propose Stephen Miller in his place. For ten years he has written the words the first beast speaks, and he speaks for him on matters at home and abroad. The reasons, the three tests of the text, and the case against are on The Three Frogs. All three frogs are graded possible, and no sign has been shown for any of them.

Where it stands. Revised; accepted November 14, 2026. I hold no reading of Mr. Johnson as a frog, and neither man's soul is judged here.

4. The second beast: the Iranian message to the vice president

As it stood. Card 9 of The Second Beast said that Iran told Vice President Vance that the president's envoys were more interested in profiting from inside knowledge of the talks than in reaching a deal.

The objection. The report rests on one unnamed Iranian official, speaking for a government at war with the United States, and the vice president denied it. By this site's own rule a claim goes "on the record" only with a named source, documents, a second outlet or an official step. This one has none, and the page stated it more firmly than the source allowed.

The revision I accept. I removed it from the evidence. It is held, off the page, and will return only if it is confirmed.

Where it stands. Removed November 14, 2026.

5. "Vote, then die"

As first written. "His condescending statement to the voters of Iowa is in reality a warning to all, vote for him and then die."

The objection. That is not what he said. On January 14, 2024, in bitter cold on the eve of the Iowa caucus, he told supporters who were sick to come out anyway: "Even if you vote and then pass away, it's worth it" (USA Today). The crowd took it as a joke. "A warning to all" was my gloss, and it turned a careless remark into a threat.

The revision I accept. I quote his words as he spoke them and withdraw the gloss. I still count the remark as a small instance of what card 5 describes, a man who puts himself first, even in jest, before the good of those who follow him. It is not a warning, and I do not use it as one.

Where it stands. Revised; accepted November 15, 2026.

6. The seven-year count

As first written. "It is likely that his first term in office completed four years of this time frame and that inauguration day (Jan 20, 2025) begins the last three years."

The objection. That sentence sets a date. The Church forbids computing the time of the end: "It is not for you to know the times or seasons" (Acts 1:7; CCC 673). The seven-year frame is not Catholic teaching, and the sentence also contradicted my own later reading, in which only part of the first term is counted.

The revision I accept. I withdraw the sentence. The numbers are now tested only backward, against what has already happened, on The Timing, where the proposal is graded possible. I count nothing forward, and I ask readers not to.

Where it stands. Withdrawn; accepted November 15, 2026.

7. "You know how they are snakes"

As first written. I quoted, under the false prophet, words that Melania Trump "allegedly said of Ivanka and her husband, Jared Kushner."

The objection. The words come from one person's account of a private remark: Stephanie Winston Wolkoff, a former adviser to Mrs. Trump, in her 2020 book Melania and Me and in interviews (Forward). Mrs. Trump has not confirmed them. And even if she said them, an insult in a family quarrel tells us nothing about Revelation 13. I used it because the word "snakes" echoed the dragon, and that is a play on a word, not evidence.

The revision I accept. I do not use it. It stays on the page as first written, as a record of where I began, and it has no place on The Second Beast.

Where it stands. Withdrawn as evidence; accepted November 15, 2026.

8. 666 Fifth Avenue

As first written. "He paid more than the value of the property at the time. It took all he had to make this purchase."

The objection. The price in 2007, $1.8 billion, was a record for a single office building, but the purchase was made by his family's company and financed largely with borrowed money. It did not take "all he had," so the parable I set beside it does not fit. And the number in Revelation 13:18 is "the number of a man," the number of a name. A street address is not a name.

The revision I accept. I withdraw "it took all he had." I keep the address only as a coincidence worth noting, not as proof of anything, and it carries no weight in any grade. The number is weighed on 666 and the Mark.

Where it stands. Revised; accepted November 15, 2026.
